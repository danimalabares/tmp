\begin{frame}{1. Definition of a deformation}
Following Huybrechts, a deformation of a compact complex
manifold $X$ is a proper smooth holomorphic family
\[
\pi:\mathcal X\longrightarrow B,\qquad
0\in B,\quad \pi^{-1}(0)\simeq X,
\]
considered near $0$. The fibres $X_t$ vary the complex structure.

A tangent vector $v\in T_0B$ is an infinitesimal direction.
The Kodaira--Spencer map turns it into a class on $X$.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{2. What is $\operatorname{Def}(X)$?}
\small
As in the HP deformation notes, first consider the
\emph{deformation functor}: it assigns to an infinitesimal
base $A$ the isomorphism classes of deformations of $X$
over $\operatorname{Spec}A$ (with the central fibre identified
with $X$):
\[
\operatorname{Def}_X:
\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb C}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Sets}.
\]
The \emph{Kuranishi space} $\operatorname{Def}(X)$ is instead
the germ of a complex analytic base carrying a
\emph{semiuniversal family}
$\mathcal X_{\mathrm{Kur}}\to\operatorname{Def}(X)$.

Every sufficiently small deformation is locally pulled back
from this family. The base-change map need not be unique,
but the semiuniversal family has exactly the right
infinitesimal parameters:
\[
T_0\operatorname{Def}(X)\ \simeq\ H^1(X,T_X).
\]
For IHS $X$, the base is smooth by BTT; this is a
\emph{local} deformation space, not a global moduli variety.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{3. Prorepresentability and the formal spectrum}
\small
The infinitesimal deformation functor
$\operatorname{Def}_X:\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbf{Sets}$
is \emph{prorepresentable} if a complete local Noetherian
$\mathbb C$-algebra $(R,\mathfrak m)$ gives natural bijections
\[
\operatorname{Def}_X(A)\cong
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{loc},\mathbb C}(R,A).
\]
Its \emph{formal spectrum} is
$\operatorname{Spf}R$: its underlying space consists of the
open prime ideals of $R$ (for the $\mathfrak m$-adic topology);
it carries the completed structure sheaf. Here it has one point,
but remembers all infinitesimal neighborhoods
$\operatorname{Spec}(R/\mathfrak m^{n+1})$.

\medskip
A formal family over $\operatorname{Spf}R$ then yields every
infinitesimal deformation by a \emph{unique} base-change map.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{4. A hull versus a representing object}
\small
Kuranishi generally constructs a \emph{semiuniversal}
deformation: a natural transformation
\[
h_R=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{loc}}(R,-)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Def}_X
\]
that is formally smooth and is an isomorphism on tangent spaces.
Every small deformation comes from it, but the inducing
map to the base need not be unique.

\medskip
\textbf{Compare the Hilbert scheme:}
the Hilbert functor of flat embedded families of closed
subschemes of a fixed projective space is globally
represented by a Hilbert scheme; every family is a
\emph{unique} pullback of the universal subscheme.
Abstract deformations of $X$ do not fix an ambient space,
and Kuranishi's construction is local.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{5. Why IHS deformations are especially nice}
\small
For an irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold,
\[
H^0(X,T_X)\simeq H^0(X,\Omega_X^1)=0.
\]
Thus there are no infinitesimal automorphisms, and the
local deformation functor is prorepresentable.
Bogomolov--Tian--Todorov additionally gives unobstructedness,
so its representing algebra is
\[
R\simeq\mathbb C[[t_1,\ldots,t_N]],
\qquad N=h^1(X,T_X)=h^{1,1}(X).
\]
Consequently $\operatorname{Spf}R$ is the formal completion
of a smooth $N$-dimensional germ, and analytically
\[
(\operatorname{Def}(X),0)\simeq(\mathbb C^N,0).
\]
This is \emph{local} representability, not a global
fine moduli variety.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{6. The relative tangent sequence}
Because $\pi$ is a submersion, its differential is surjective.
The kernel consists of vectors tangent to the fibres:
\[
0\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal X/B}
\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal X}
\xrightarrow{d\pi}\pi^*T_B\longrightarrow0.
\]
Restrict to the central fibre. Since
$T_{\mathcal X/B}|_X=T_X$ and
$\pi^*T_B|_X=T_0B\otimes\mathcal O_X$, we obtain
\[
\boxed{0\longrightarrow T_X\longrightarrow
T_{\mathcal X}|_X\longrightarrow
T_0B\otimes\mathcal O_X\longrightarrow0.}
\]
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{7. The connecting homomorphism trick}
Apply the long exact sequence of sheaf cohomology:
\[
H^0(X,T_{\mathcal X}|_X)\longrightarrow
\underbrace{H^0(X,T_0B\otimes\mathcal O_X)}_{=\,T_0B}
\xrightarrow{\ \delta\ }H^1(X,T_X)\longrightarrow\cdots
\]
Here $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=\mathbb C$ for compact connected $X$.
The connecting map is precisely Kodaira--Spencer:
\[
\boxed{\rho=\delta:T_0B\longrightarrow H^1(X,T_X).}
\]
A local lift of $v$ to $T_{\mathcal X}|_X$ need not glue
globally. Differences of local lifts are tangent to $X$
and define a Cech $1$-cocycle representing $\rho(v)$.
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{8. The transition-function picture (HP)}
Choose local trivialisations with changes of coordinates
$(x,t)\mapsto(\psi_{ij}(x,t),t)$.
For $v\in T_0B$, differentiating at $t=0$ gives
\[
\theta_{ij}=\left.\frac d{d\varepsilon}\right|_0
\psi_{ij}(x,\varepsilon v).
\]
These vector fields give a Cech $1$-cocycle;
changing trivialisations changes it by a coboundary.
Thus $\rho(v)=[\theta_{ij}]\in H^1(X,T_X)$.
\smallskip

\texttt{complex-geometry.tex}, relative tangent sequence.}
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{9. The IHS computation}
Dolbeault gives $H^1(X,T_X)\simeq
H_{\bar\partial}^{0,1}(X,T^{1,0}X)$.
For an IHS manifold, contraction with the nondegenerate
holomorphic symplectic form $\sigma$ is an isomorphism
\[
T_X\xrightarrow{\ \iota_{(-)}\sigma\ }\Omega_X^1.
\]
Hence, by Dolbeault and the Kahler Hodge decomposition,
\[
\boxed{T_0\operatorname{Def}(X)
\simeq H^1(X,T_X)\simeq H^1(X,\Omega_X^1)
\simeq H^{1,1}(X).}
\]
The Bogomolov--Tian--Todorov theorem ensures that
$\operatorname{Def}(X)$ is smooth.
\end{frame}
